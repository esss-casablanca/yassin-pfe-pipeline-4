*Original title (French): Satisfaction au travail et intention de rester chez les infirmiers en Afrique du Nord et au Moyen-Orient : revue systématique.*

## Abstract

**Background.** Nurse retention is a major challenge for health systems in the North Africa and Middle East region. **Objective.** To synthesise the studies that measured the association between job satisfaction and intention to stay (or to leave) among nurses of the region. **Methods.** Systematic review conducted according to PRISMA 2020; searches of MEDLINE, Scopus and CINAHL from 1 January 2010 to 31 March 2026; quantitative observational studies; quality appraisal with the JBI tool. **Results.** Of 1,248 records identified, 23 studies were included (total n = 9,874 nurses). Twenty-one studies (91.3%) report a positive association between overall satisfaction and intention to stay; intrinsic satisfaction is the dimension most frequently associated (15 studies). No Moroccan study measured the association in the public sector with a validated instrument. **Conclusion.** The evidence suggests a robust but exclusively cross-sectional association; the Moroccan gap justifies the empirical study of Component 2.

**Keywords:** job satisfaction; intention to stay; nurses; retention; systematic review.

## Introduction

The nursing shortage affects most countries of the North Africa and Middle East region [1,2]. In Morocco, nurse density is estimated at 1.0 per 1,000 inhabitants, one of the lowest in the region [3]. Job satisfaction is regarded as a modifiable determinant of intention to stay [4,5], but existing syntheses focus mainly on North America and Europe [6]. This review addresses the question: among nurses of the region, is job satisfaction associated with intention to stay?

## Methods

The review followed the PRISMA 2020 recommendations [7]. We searched MEDLINE, Scopus and CINAHL from 1 January 2010 to 31 March 2026, with no language restriction. Inclusion criteria were: practising nurses; quantitative measurement of job satisfaction and of intention to stay or to leave; countries of the region. Two reviewers selected the studies independently; disagreements were resolved by consensus. Methodological quality was appraised with the JBI checklist for cross-sectional studies [8]. Because of the heterogeneity of instruments, a narrative synthesis was performed.

## Results

The search identified 1,248 records; after removal of 312 duplicates, 936 titles and abstracts were screened, 71 full texts assessed and 23 studies included (Figure 1). The studies came from 9 countries; 4 were Moroccan, all conducted in the private sector or with a non-validated instrument. The cumulative sample was 9,874 nurses; the median sample size was 312 (range 98–1,450).

Twenty-one studies (91.3%) report a positive and statistically significant association between overall satisfaction and intention to stay; two studies found no association. Intrinsic satisfaction (recognition, autonomy, meaning of work) is the dimension most often associated (15 studies), ahead of extrinsic satisfaction (9 studies). The reported odds ratios range from 1.3 to 3.2.

## Discussion

The results suggest a robust association between job satisfaction and intention to stay in the region, consistent with international syntheses [6]. However, all studies are cross-sectional, which precludes any causal inference, and 6 studies (26.1%) used non-validated instruments. The main gap identified is the absence of a Moroccan public-sector study with a validated instrument: this is precisely the gap that Component 2 of this project sets out to fill.

## Conclusion

Job satisfaction is associated with intention to stay among nurses of the region, but the evidence is cross-sectional and heterogeneous. A Moroccan public-sector study with a validated instrument is needed.
