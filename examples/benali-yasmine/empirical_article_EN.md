*Original title (French): Satisfaction au travail et intention de rester chez les infirmiers du secteur public à Casablanca : étude transversale analytique.*

## Abstract

**Objective.** To measure the association between job satisfaction and intention to stay in the profession among public-sector nurses in Casablanca. **Methods.** Analytical cross-sectional study reported according to STROBE, conducted from 3 February to 28 March 2026 in three public hospitals. Satisfaction was measured with the Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire short form (MSQ-SF, 20 items); intention to stay with a 3-item scale. Logistic regression adjusted for age, sex, seniority and ward. **Results.** Of 242 nurses approached, 150 participated (response rate 62.0%). Mean overall satisfaction was 3.4 (SD 0.6) out of 5; 58.0% of participants reported a high intention to stay. Overall satisfaction was associated with intention to stay (adjusted OR = 1.42; 95% CI: 1.10–1.83; p = 0.008). **Conclusion.** Among public-sector nurses in Casablanca, job satisfaction is associated with intention to stay; this finding suggests that the levers of intrinsic satisfaction deserve managers’ attention.

**Keywords:** job satisfaction; intention to stay; nurses; public sector; Morocco.

## Introduction

The systematic review conducted in Component 1 of this project showed that job satisfaction is associated with intention to stay among nurses of the region, but that no Moroccan study had measured this association in the public sector with a validated instrument [1]. In Morocco, where nurse density is 1.0 per 1,000 inhabitants [2], retention is a declared priority [3]. The primary objective of this study was to measure the association between job satisfaction and intention to stay in the profession; the secondary objectives were to describe the level of satisfaction and to explore differences between wards.

## Methods

This was an analytical cross-sectional study, reported according to the STROBE recommendation [4], conducted from 3 February to 28 March 2026 in three public hospitals in Casablanca. State-registered nurses in post for at least 6 months were eligible; students and non-clinical managers were excluded. The planned sample size was 150 participants to detect an odds ratio of 1.5 with 80% power and a 5% alpha risk, assuming a 50% prevalence of high intention to stay.

Job satisfaction was measured with the Minnesota Satisfaction Questionnaire short form (MSQ-SF, 20 items, scale 1 to 5) in its authorised French translation [5]; intention to stay in the profession with a 3-item scale (score 3–15, high intention if ≥ 12) [6]. The common-core variables (age, sex, seniority, ward, status) were collected. The analysis comprised a description, estimation of reliability (Cronbach’s alpha) and a logistic regression of high intention to stay on overall satisfaction (per point), adjusted for age, sex, seniority and ward. The study received the ESSS ethics-exemption attestation and the authorisations of the three institutions; participation was anonymous and voluntary.

## Results

Of 242 nurses approached, 150 participated (response rate 62.0%); no questionnaire was excluded (Figure 1). The sample comprised 98 women (65.3%); mean age was 34.2 years (SD 8.1) and median seniority 9 years (IQR 4–16). Table 1 presents the characteristics of the participants.

The internal consistency of the MSQ-SF was satisfactory (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.89). Mean overall satisfaction was 3.4 (SD 0.6); mean intrinsic satisfaction 3.6 (SD 0.7) and extrinsic satisfaction 3.1 (SD 0.8). A high intention to stay was reported by 87 participants (58.0%).

Overall satisfaction was associated with high intention to stay (adjusted OR = 1.42; 95% CI: 1.10–1.83; p = 0.008). In an exploratory analysis not planned in the protocol, the proportion of high intention to stay was lower in emergency and intensive care (47.1%) than in medical wards (65.4%) (p = 0.04); this finding should be regarded as exploratory.

## Discussion

This study is, to our knowledge, the first to measure the association between job satisfaction and intention to stay among Moroccan public-sector nurses with a validated instrument. The observed association (adjusted OR = 1.42) lies within the range reported by the Component 1 review (1.3 to 3.2) [1]. The level of intrinsic satisfaction above the extrinsic level is consistent with regional studies [7].

The limitations are those of a cross-sectional design: no causal inference is possible; self-reported measures expose to common-method bias; the sample, limited to three hospitals in Casablanca, does not allow generalisation to the whole public sector; the 62.0% response rate exposes to participation bias. The finding by ward is exploratory and requires confirmation.

## Conclusion

Among public-sector nurses in Casablanca, job satisfaction is associated with intention to stay. This finding suggests that the levers of intrinsic satisfaction — recognition, autonomy, meaning of work — deserve managers’ attention; a longitudinal study is needed to establish the direction of the relationship.
